# Design System: Agwood Mill & Lumber — Stated Flat

## Overview

**North star: every field is a flat declaration of what the mill will do.**

The site is a stack of hard-edged, full-bleed bands in the company's own two
greens and its paper. A field makes one point. Where that point is a set of
related narrowings — one species, one channel, one specification — it is carried
by a three-cell row rather than three separate fields, because they answer the
same question and belong at the same altitude.

### What it forces

1. **A field makes one point.** It may be carried by one statement or by the
   three-cell row, and by nothing else. A field with two unrelated things in it
   has stopped being a declaration.
2. **Colour is the division.** There are no rules, borders, or dividers between
   fields — the colour change is the section break. A hairline between two fields
   would be admitting the colour is not doing its job.
3. **Colour is structural, not decorative.** The two greens are measured out of
   the logo. They do the work that photography does on other lumber sites.
4. **No ornament of any kind.** No texture, no pattern, no generated material, no
   illustration. See the history below — this is the load-bearing rule.

### The rule this world was built to obey

A previous direction for this site was built and rejected. It generated drawn
redwood grain — cathedral arches, grain bands, drawn placeholder panels — as its
primary visual material. The client's verdict was that it "feel[s] cheesy and
poorly executed," after four rounds of refining the geometry.

The lesson is not that the grain was drawn badly. It is that **on a site with six
confirmed facts, ornament reads as compensation.** It looks like something is
being covered up, because something is. The replacement makes the few real facts
carry the page and lets type, colour and structure do everything else.

**That rejected vocabulary is dead.** No drawn grain, no arches, no generated
texture, no wood imagery. Do not reintroduce any of it in a softened form.

### Anti-references — both named, deliberately

**1. The generic lumber-company template.** Full-bleed forest photo with the name
centred over it, a "quality and service since 19XX" block, three icon cards, a
contact form. The Webflow scaffold in this repo already is that page. This world
refuses it structurally: there is no photography, no founding year we can print,
and a three-card row is banned outright below.

**2. Schmidbauer Lumber — "The Line."** Built by the same studio, also a North
Coast redwood mill, which makes borrowing easy and the result worthless. That site
is a graphite control room about material moving through stations. This one is a
warm, flat, declarative stack. Specifically banned, each being a device that would
work unchanged on both sites: the dark cool ground, any monospace face, station
rows, the conveyor rail, instrument strips, hairline-and-tick-mark framing, and
gold in any role.

`forest` and `forest-deep` are measured, not chosen. The logo's opaque pixels
cluster into two inks: a fill green with median `#005314` and an outline green
averaging `#05240B`. Every Webflow `:root` swatch in the original scaffold —
including `--forest-green: #25681d` — was discarded; none matched the mark.

### Named rule: **Green acts, russet locates**

Green appears **only** on something you can do: the action, inline links, focus
rings. Russet appears **only** on where you are or what is missing: the current
nav item, the statement numerals, an unknown value, the `Pending` marker.

**They never cross.** Green never marks position. Russet never invites a click.

On the dark field both take reversed variants (`on-deep`, `on-deep-mark`) because
neither is legible at `#05240B`. The meaning does not change — only the value.

**Don'ts for this rule specifically:**
- Don't use green for a heading, an eyebrow, a border, or a bullet.
- Don't use green to show which page you are on.
- Don't use russet on a button, a link, or anything else clickable.
- Don't add a third accent for warnings, success, or emphasis. There is none. An
  unknown is russet because "we don't know yet" is a state.
- Don't use `forest` as a field background. The dark field is `forest-deep`. Two
  greens doing the same job is how the palette starts drifting.

### Contrast — verified, not assumed

**Every text colour was checked against every ground it actually sits on**, by
computation against the shipped tokens rather than by eye. Fourteen pairings, all
clearing WCAG 2.1 AA:

| | on `paper` | on `paper-deep` | on `forest-deep` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ink` / `on-deep` | 14.67:1 | 12.94:1 | 14.30:1 |
| `ink-dim` / `on-deep-dim` | 6.03:1 | 5.32:1 | 10.23:1 |
| `forest` | 7.88:1 | 6.95:1 | — (inverts to `on-deep`) |
| `russet` / `on-deep-mark` | 5.79:1 | 5.11:1 | 7.49:1 |

`paper` on `forest` (the action) is 7.88:1, rising to 10.46:1 on hover. The
tightest pair on the site is **russet on `paper-deep` at 5.11:1**. `#9A4E2A`, the
more attractive russet, failed at 4.44:1 and was rejected.

The action on the dark field inverts, and its pressed state is the one case where
a pressed state *lightens* — on `forest-deep` the only direction away from the
ground is brighter. Contrast still rises rather than falls: `forest-deep` on
`on-deep-press` is **16.62:1**, up from 14.30:1 on `on-deep`.

## Typography

**Two families, both variable, every axis set explicitly.**

**Fraunces** carries display, headline and title — with `SOFT 0` and `WONK 0` set
deliberately. Those axes are what give Fraunces its wobble; switching them off
leaves a sharp, high-contrast serif. This is the single most important type
decision in the system and it is invisible unless you know to look.

**Archivo** carries lede, body, label and control at `wdth 100` (105 for labels,
which need to hold their tracking).

**There is no monospace anywhere.** A grade stamp is the obvious thing to reach
for on a lumber site and it leads straight to mono technical labels — which is
Schmidbauer's vocabulary, not this one.

## Layout

**One shell, one gutter, one container width.** A `Field` is a full-bleed
background wrapper; the `.shell` inside owns the width and the gutter. The wrapper
never sets a max-width — doing that is what creates two competing widths.

- Gutter: `clamp(1.125rem, 4vw, 2rem)`
- Field padding: `clamp(3rem, 8vw, 6rem)`, with `tight` at ×0.62 and `tall` at ×1.45
- Container: `68rem` · Measure: `56ch`

### Named rule: **the dark field is a bookend, not a beat**

Fields alternate so the page never places two of the same tone together, and the
sequence bookends on `forest-deep`: the header and hero open dark, the footer
closes dark. The bands in between alternate `paper` and `paper-deep`.

**Do not run `forest-deep` as a mid-page band.** It was tried on 2026-08-27 —
making the dark field every other band, on the argument that `paper` to
`paper-deep` is only a ~6% luminance step and therefore too quiet to be a section
division. The result was rejected on sight: it stripes the page
green/white/green/white, which reads as a pattern imposed on the content rather
than as sections divided by colour. The quiet step is the point. A section break
that announces itself every time is a rhythm the reader starts counting instead of
reading.

**Two consecutive `forest-deep` fields are allowed in exactly one case:** when the
second elaborates the first and they are meant to read as one uninterrupted band,
which is what the header and hero already do. The About page's ownership record
sits directly under the hero and extends that opening band. It is not licence to
run a dark field anywhere else.

**Vertical rhythm is not uniform.** One padding value applied to every field is a
rhythm nobody can feel. A field carrying the page's argument takes `tall`; a
closing line takes `tight`. The hero takes a `min-height` in `svh` — never `vh`,
which resizes when mobile browser chrome appears.

## Elevation & Depth

**There are no shadows.** Not one. Depth comes from the tonal step between fields
and nothing else. A panel that casts a shadow is a card, a card wants a grid, and
a grid wants three of them — which is how the category template gets back in.

## Shapes

**Radius is `0` everywhere**, including the action and the `Pending` block. The
`Pending` marker and the dealer rows use a 4px left edge in the state colour; that
edge is the only border in the system that is not a row hairline.

## Components

Full specifications live in `.impeccable/design.json`. Eight are signature.

**What makes something a signature here, and the bar a new one has to clear.** This
site has roughly six confirmed facts, and ornament on a page that thin reads as
compensation — that is the founding lesson of this world and it has not changed. So
every extra device carries *information a buyer wants* and nothing else does. The
test each one below passes: **would this device work unchanged on Schmidbauer?** If
yes, it is wrong for this site. That is not a style rule, it is the reason the
device exists — a thing that could be lifted onto another mill's site is decoration
wearing a fact's clothes.

### The Field (signature)

A full-bleed band of one colour carrying exactly one statement. The colour change
between consecutive fields is the only section division on the site. `tone` is
`paper`, `deep` or `forest`; the dark tone flips every child to reversed tokens.

### The Cell Row (signature)

Three cells on one field, each carrying a small label in the state colour, a
Fraunces argument, supporting prose, and the fact the argument resolves to. The
facts are bottom-aligned across the row so they line up regardless of how long the
arguments above them run.

This is the closest the site comes to the icon-card triad the category runs on,
and the distance is held by three things: the cells have no box of any kind, each
carries a checkable claim rather than a benefit, and there are three because there
are three things to say. The conditions are written as Don'ts below and are not
negotiable — they are the whole reason this row is allowed.

### Named rule: **a drawing must answer a question, not fill a space**

The ornament ban above is absolute and is not softened by anything here. What
separates the drawings this site does carry from the drawn grain it threw away is
not subject matter or restraint — it is whether a reader **acts differently** for
having seen it. The reach map answers "how far is this mill from my yard." The
heartwood section answers "what does the word Heart in a grade name mean." Take
either away and a real question goes unanswered.

A flow diagram of the wholesale channel was built to this brief on 2026-08-27 and
removed the same day. It passed the tests on paper and still did not earn its place:
the copy beside it already said "we do not sell direct" in one sentence, and a
picture that restates a sentence is decoration however defensible its reasoning.
Passing the tests is necessary, not sufficient.

Three tests, all of which a new drawing has to pass:

1. **Name the question it answers**, in the words a buyer would use. If the answer is
   "it makes the page feel less empty", it is ornament and this world already
   rejected it once.
2. **Would it work unchanged on Schmidbauer?** If yes it is wrong here — see the
   anti-references.
3. **Does the same information survive in text?** Every figure carries a real
   `figcaption` stating its fact in words. A drawing may be the best way to see
   something; it may never be the only place it exists.

And two rules of construction. **No glyph ever stands in for a mark** — arrowheads,
squares and rules are drawn geometry, and the moment a cross or a tick appears the
icon vocabulary is back. **Arrowheads only where direction is the information**: on a
flow, yes; on a leader line pointing at a label, they are decoration.

### The Emblem (signature)

The company mark, vector since 2026-08-27, traced from the delivered logo's alpha
channel by `scripts/trace-emblem.mjs` and regenerable from it. It draws with
`fill="currentColor"`, so there is no separate light or dark asset to keep in step
with the palette.

The 64px legibility floor **still applies**. It was measured on the raster at 44, 56,
64, 72 and 88, and below 64 the conifer branches merge into a blob — that is the
mark's own geometry, not a resolution limit, so going vector did not lift it. The
footer had been calling it at 46px since it was built; that is corrected.

### The Heartwood Section (signature)

A photograph of a redwood log sawn across, with the zones **annotated rather than
redrawn**. It carries the one fact that decodes every redwood grade label: heartwood
resists decay and insects, sapwood does not, and **every heartwood grade has
"Heart" in its name.** One board is outlined across the boundary, because where the
cut falls is what decides the grade — that rectangle is what makes it a milling
figure instead of a botany one.

An earlier version drew two shaded circles. That was a diagram of an idea; this is
the specimen, and the boundary is already visible in it. **Nothing imposes a perfect
circle on a real log's irregular one** — the overlay points, it does not trace. That
is the difference between annotation and the drawn material this world rejected.

The annotation coordinates are **measured, not eyeballed**: heartwood to r≈108 from
centre (162,181), sapwood band r≈112–142, and every leader target and board corner
was checked against the pixel beneath it. They are coordinates into *that* image and
must be re-measured if the asset is replaced.

Drawn in full `ink`, never `ink-dim`, which measures 1.6:1 over the heartwood and
disappears. Ink clears 3.9:1 on the darkest wood in frame and 9.2:1 on the palest.

**The photograph lives inside the SVG**, one element and one coordinate system. The
first build layered an absolutely-positioned overlay on an `<img>`, so the annotation
only landed while three separate things held — a percentage width, `position:
absolute`, and two aspect ratios agreeing. That is not a failure mode that degrades
gracefully: lose any one and the labels detach and stack below the photograph
pointing at nothing, which is exactly what happened in dev when a stylesheet went
stale. Keep the image in the SVG.

It describes the **species, never Agwood's stock**, whose grades are an `UNKNOWN`,
and claims **no proportion**. Both limits are written into the component.

### The Reach Map (signature)

The northern half of California drawn from real boundary data, with Ukiah marked
and straight-line distances to seven reference towns computed from coordinates.
Generated at build time; the browser receives static SVG.

It is the one graphic on the site, and it earns its place by being information
rather than decoration: a lumber buyer thinks in logistics before grades, and
"how far is this mill from my yard" is a question this site can answer exactly on
a page where very little else can be. The towns are **reference points, not
customers** — Agwood sells "across Northern California", and naming towns as
destinations would claim delivery relationships nobody has described to us.

The outline is never to be redrawn by hand. A recognisable shape approximated
from memory is a factual error readers spot instantly.

### The Phone Display (signature)

The telephone number set as the largest element on the page. The telephone is the
only way into this business — there is no form and no email address — so the
number wears the size that importance warrants rather than hiding in a button.
Tabular figures, because proportional numerals in a serif this high-contrast make
a phone number look accidental.

It takes the `display` step itself rather than a clamp sitting just under it, so
it moves when the scale moves. A hard-coded size here quietly became the
second-largest thing on the page the moment the display step was raised.

### The Open Sign (signature)

Whether the mill office is answering the telephone *right now*, computed against
`America/Los_Angeles` — the mill's clock, never the visitor's.

It earns its place because the telephone is the **only** channel this business
has. There is no form and no email address, by the client's decision, so the
alternative to a live call is not a slower channel — it is no channel. This is the
one thing the site can tell a buyer that it could not tell them on paper.

Rendered server-side as the plain hours line and upgraded in place, so a visitor
without scripting reads the same information at lower resolution rather than a
blank. An engine without the timezone database bails out and leaves the static
hours standing: a wrong live answer is worse than no live answer.

**Open and closed take the same colour.** Both are states, and russet is the state
colour. Colouring "open" green would break the palette rule twice — green marks
actions, never states, and there is deliberately no third accent for success. The
two are told apart by the words and by a filled versus hollow mark. The mark is a
square, because radius is `0` everywhere and a lone circle would be the only round
thing on the site.

### The Ownership Record (signature)

Three dated beats: the name going on a Ukiah mill in the early 1980s, the 2017
sale to an Oregon company, the 2025 restart under local family ownership.

It answers the question this site's **second job** exists for. The public record
is contradictory in documented ways and several live listings describe the
predecessor company; a buyer who lands here off a stale listing is asking "is this
the same outfit, and is it running?" and nothing else on the site answers it.

The last beat takes the state colour because russet marks **where you are**, and on
a chronology where you are is the present. 2025 is not marked because it is good
news; it is marked because it is the row the reader is standing in.

**Not numbered.** The dates already carry the sequence, a numeral on top of a date
is decoration on top of information, and a numbered sequence is the signature
device of the site this one must not resemble. An ordered list is nonetheless the
correct semantics: this is a chronology and the order is the meaning.

**The dates are not the banned founding year.** That ban targets "Since 1954",
which appears only in draft copy and is contradicted by three independent sources.
These have recorded provenance. They may not be rewritten into a heritage claim —
"milling redwood since 1981" is precisely the sentence the evidence does not
support. Ships behind `SHOW_RECORD` in `src/data/company.ts` pending client
sign-off.

## Motion

**One authored moment, then quiet.** The site's motion motif is a flat colour
field being laid down, and it appears in exactly two places:

1. **The hero wipes itself on.** Its lines are painted upward with a `clip-path`
   inset, staggered about 100ms apart. This is a load animation and needs no
   script. `animation-fill-mode` is `backwards`, never `forwards`, so once the run
   ends the element carries no `clip-path` at all — a clip left applied would crop
   the focus ring on the hero's own call to action.
2. **The action's pressed state** is the darker green wiped up over the lighter
   one, `scaleY` from the bottom edge. Same gesture, quarter the duration.

Below the fold, fields reveal on scroll with a short fade and rise, and grouped
children — a cell row, the two halves of a split — trail their field by ~90ms so a
row of three arrives as a row. **Those children carry a transform and never an
opacity:** the field above already owns visibility, so if the reveal class never
lands the worst case is a 12px offset rather than invisible content.

The previous version ran one identical fade-and-rise on every field, which is the
web's default gesture and therefore reads as no gesture at all.

Hidden start states live under `:root.js` only, so a visitor without scripting
sees everything. `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` reveals content **immediately**
rather than leaving it hidden, and cancels the hero animation outright. Scroll
state is observed with a single `IntersectionObserver`, never a scroll handler.

## Browser surfaces

The parts the stylesheet does not draw still belong to the design. Selection takes
the state colour's ground and flips to `on-deep-mark` inside a dark field; the
scrollbar is themed from `ink-dim` on `paper-deep`; `scroll-padding-top` clears the
sticky header so an in-page jump — the skip link above all — never lands its target
underneath the bar.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Give each field one point, carried by prose, a record, or the three-cell row.
- Bottom-align the facts across a cell row so they read as a set, and set the fact
  **larger** than the argument above it — the fact is the payload of that device.
- Let the colour change be the section break, and keep the dark field as the
  page's bookends — never as a mid-page band.
- Vary the vertical: `tall` for a field making the argument, `tight` for a closing
  line.
- Make a new device carry information a buyer wants, and check it against the
  Schmidbauer test before building it.
- Give every figure a `figcaption` that states its fact in words.
- Draw every mark as geometry. Arrowheads only where direction is the information.
- Set `SOFT 0` and `WONK 0` on every Fraunces instance.
- Use green for actions and russet for state, and nothing else for either.
- Demote an unknown to Archivo in russet so it cannot read as a held fact.
- Keep the emblem at **64px or taller** — measured at 44, 56, 64, 72 and 88; below
  64 the conifer branches merge and the monogram stops reading.
- Set the company name in live type. The wordmark baked into the raster is never
  used.
- Check any new colour pair against its actual ground before shipping it.

### Don't

- **Don't reintroduce ornament in any form.** No texture, no pattern, no generated
  material, no wood imagery. This world exists because that was rejected.
- **Don't put two unrelated points in one field.**
- **Don't turn the three-cell row into an icon-card triad.** No icons, no cards,
  no borders around cells, no shadows, no radius, no per-cell background. The
  cells sit on the field and are separated by one hairline. The moment a cell gets
  a box, the row becomes the category template's load-bearing device and the whole
  structure goes with it.
- **Don't add a fourth cell.** Three is the count because there are exactly three
  things to say. A fourth is the signal the row has stopped being a structure and
  become a container — split it into fields instead.
- **Don't put a benefit in a cell.** A cell carries an argument and the fact it
  resolves to, both checkable. "One species, run properly — Redwood only" is a
  claim a buyer can test; "Quality Craftsmanship" is what this site exists to
  avoid.
- **Don't draw a rule between two fields.** The colour change is the division.
- **Don't run `forest-deep` between two light fields.** The dark field opens and
  closes the page. Striping it through the middle was built and rejected.
- **Don't add a second accent for "open", "success" or "warning".** There are two
  colours and they do not cross. A state that needs distinguishing from another
  state is distinguished by words and by fill, not by a new hue.
- **Don't number a sequence** when the sequence already carries its own labels. A
  numeral on top of a date is decoration on top of information — and it is the
  other mill site's device.
- **Don't draw a graphic that only fills space.** Name the question it answers first;
  if you cannot, it is the ornament this world was built to refuse.
- **Don't reach for russet to colour a material.** Redwood heartwood is brown and
  russet is not available for it. Separate materials by value.
- **Don't put a glyph in a diagram** — no crosses, ticks or arrows-as-characters.
  Every mark is drawn geometry, or the icon vocabulary is back.
- **Don't set a local `font-size` that sits just under a token.** It opts that
  element out of the scale silently, and it stops moving when the scale moves. Use
  the step, or add one and record it here.
- **Don't add a shadow**, and don't round a corner.
- **Don't use a monospace face** anywhere.
- **Don't hero a photograph**, and don't let any image or caption imply Agwood owns
  a stand of trees, a yard, or equipment it has not shown us.
- **Don't print a founding year.** "Since 1954" appears only in draft copy and is
  contradicted by three independent sources. See `PRODUCT.md`.
- **Don't write a sustainability or certification claim.** Agwood holds no FSC or
  SFI certification. The California Forest Practice Act may be stated as
  jurisdictional fact only, never as Agwood's practice or credit.
- **Don't let a field wrapper set its own max-width.** The `.shell` inside owns the
  width; a second one produces nested insets.
- **Don't reuse anything from Schmidbauer.** If a device would work unchanged on
  both sites, it is wrong for this one.
